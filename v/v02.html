Reproduced 9/9

It knew the lesion was there. It wouldn't say where.

Two years of reported performance, and nobody asked the model the one question that mattered. Ask it where the tumour is and it points at half the hemisphere. The smaller the lesion, the wider it misses.

The Journal

Four leads

Lead 01 — the data

They told me small lesions are just hard. A few voxels, no contrast, borders nobody agrees on. Convenient. So I took the same lesions and handed them to a plain supervised U‑Net. No language. No prompt. Nothing clever.

It degraded 1.2×. The thing I was investigating degrades 14.9× on the same tissue.

The lesions are findable. Somebody just wasn't finding them.

Lead dead — 1.2× vs 14.9×

Lead 02 — the language

Figured the text encoder was weak. That would have been a clean answer and an easy fix. So I pulled PubMedBERT out and dropped in random vectors. No medical knowledge. No semantics. Noise.

Nothing changed. the prompt was never being read The query is an opaque class identifier that happens to be spelled in English.

It isn't reading the prompt. It's looking up a channel.

Lead dead — Δ ≈ 0.000

Lead 03 — the clever fixes

Three of them. Tell the model the lesion is small. Ensemble across scales. Retrain with the sizes rebalanced. Every one of them worked until I ran the control, and then every one of them came apart.

Helped small, broke large. Gained the core, lost the enhancing. Overfit the exact tercile it was tuned on. All three reversed.

Lead dead — 3 of 3 reversed

Lead 04 — the plumbing

It was never the model. It was never the language. The volume gets walked in overlapping windows and the windows get averaged flat, which smears a small lesion across everything it touches on the way past.

Weight each window toward its own centre. That's it. That's the whole fix. +0.145 Dice and not one weight changed.

Confirmed — 0 → 7 of 21 subjects
