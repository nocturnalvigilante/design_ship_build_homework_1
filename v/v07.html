Medical imaging  /  Vision and language

The model that could not point.

Ask a text-conditioned 3D model whether there is a tumour and it will tell you, correctly. Ask it where, and once the lesion is small, it hands back a region many times too large and centred somewhere else entirely.

Raj Hansini AkaraMPCS 53113 · University of Chicago
BraTS2020 · 369 patients

The clinically urgent lesions are the small ones. They are the ones a radiologist might miss on a busy afternoon, the ones that argue for another scan in three months rather than six. They are also, it turns out, precisely the ones these models cannot find.

Not detect — find. The distinction matters more than it sounds. Run the model on a held-out patient with a small enhancing tumour and it will confidently report that a lesion is present. It will then outline a region roughly sixteen times the true volume, its centre a couple of centimetres from where the tumour actually sits.

“Localization degrades monotonically with lesion size, in every region, with no recovery.”

Not the data

The obvious objection is that small lesions are simply hard. A few voxels, low contrast, ambiguous borders — perhaps nothing can find them. So the same small lesions were handed to a plain supervised U-Net, no text involved. It degraded by a factor of 1.2, against 14.9 for the text-conditioned model on enhancing tumour.

The lesions are learnable. Something about conditioning on language is what breaks.

Not the language either

The next suspicion was the text encoder, and the fix would have been a better one. Instead, PubMedBERT was replaced with random vectors — no medical knowledge, no semantics, nothing. Performance barely moved.

Which yields the paper's sharpest line: the query functions as an opaque class identifier that happens to be spelled in English. The model is not reading the prompt. It is looking up a channel.

The cheapest thing on the list

Three elaborate interventions were tried. Size-conditioned prompting helped small lesions and broke large ones. Multi-scale ensembling gained on tumour core and gave the gain back on enhancing tumour. Scale-matched retraining overfit the size tercile it was tuned on. Each was reversed after a control caught the error.

What worked was the least interesting idea available. The model processes volumes in overlapping sliding windows and averages them flatly, which smears a small lesion sitting near a window edge across everything around it. Weight each window toward its own centre instead, and Dice rises by 0.145 with no retraining whatsoever. Small enhancing tumour goes from nought to seven of twenty-one patients.

14.9×Dice degradation from large lesions to small, enhancing tumour. The supervised control, on the same lesions, degrades 1.2×.

A shared confound is not a cancelled confound.

Three results here were reversed after the fact, each time by a control that should have been run first. The lesson generalises past this dataset: every stage of a pipeline is an instrument until somebody measures it.
