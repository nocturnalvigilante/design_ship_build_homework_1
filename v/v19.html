IT FINDStheLESION ITCAN'TPLACEIT

Text-conditioned 3D medical models say yes there's a tumour and then point at half the hemisphere. The smaller the lesion the wider they miss. Nobody checked. For two years.

Exhibit A — the scene

Sixteen times the volume. Two centimetres off. Dice 0.037. Detection: perfect. That's the whole scam.

14.9×

WORSE on small lesions than large. Monotonic in every region. Never recovers.

← and the small ones are the ones that actually matter

They said: the lesions are too small

We gave the same lesions to a plain supervised U-Net. No language. No prompt. It degraded 1.2×.

LIE — 1.2 vs 14.9

They said: the encoder is weak

We ripped out PubMedBERT and shoved in random vectors. Noise. Nothing changed. The query is an opaque class id that happens to be spelled in English.

LIE — Δ ≈ 0

We tried the clever stuff

Size-conditioned prompting. Multi-scale ensembling. Scale-matched retraining.

Every one of them worked until we ran the control. All three reversed. We printed it anyway.

It was the plumbing the whole time

The volume gets walked in overlapping windows and the windows get averaged flat, which smears a small lesion over everything it touches on the way past. Weight each window toward its own centre. That's it.
