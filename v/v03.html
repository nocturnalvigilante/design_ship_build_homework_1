Diagnosing Small-Lesion Grounding Failure in Text-Conditioned 3D Medical Localization

Raj Hansini Akara

University of Chicago

MPCS 53113

Abstract

Text-conditioned 3D medical localization models report strong aggregate performance while failing at the task they are deployed for. We show that localization degrades monotonically with lesion size, by up to 14.9× in Dice from the largest size tercile to the smallest, while detection remains unaffected. Three controls isolate the cause. A supervised U-Net given the same small lesions degrades only 1.2×, establishing that the lesions are learnable. Replacing the domain text encoder with random vectors changes performance by ≈0.000 Dice, establishing that the language contributes nothing measurable: the query functions as an opaque class identifier that happens to be spelled in English. Three size-aware interventions each traded one failure mode for another and were reversed under control. The dominant failure is instead located in inference-time window merging; reweighting each sliding window toward its centre yields +0.145 Dice with no retraining, recovering small enhancing tumour in 7 of 21 patients where the model previously found none. All results are reported over 171 paired Wilcoxon tests with Benjamini‑Hochberg correction and replicate in 9 of 9 comparisons across three independent splits.

1. Introduction

The clinically consequential lesions are the small ones. They are the findings a radiologist is most likely to miss, and the ones that change a follow-up interval from six months to three. They are also, we find, precisely the regime in which text-conditioned 3D localization fails.

The distinction we draw is between detection and localization. Queried on a held-out patient with a small enhancing tumour, the model under study reports the presence of a lesion correctly. It then returns a region approximately sixteen times the true volume, displaced roughly two centimetres from the lesion centroid (Fig. 1). Aggregate metrics computed over an unstratified test set do not surface this behaviour.

We contribute: (i) a size-stratified evaluation establishing that the failure is monotonic and present in every region; (ii) two controls that exclude the data and the language respectively as explanations; and (iii) an inference-time repair that outperforms three more elaborate training-time interventions at zero retraining cost.

2. Size-stratified evaluation

We partition the held-out set (n = 73) into terciles by lesion volume and report Dice per region in Table 1. The decline is monotonic in all three regions and does not recover. Enhancing tumour, the smallest and most clinically specific region, falls 14.9× from the large tercile to the small.

3. Controls

3.1. The lesions are learnable

A plain supervised U-Net trained on the same volumes, with no language conditioning at any stage, degrades by 1.2× over the same size range. The small-lesion regime is therefore not intrinsically unlearnable, and the degradation cannot be attributed to the data.

3.2. The language contributes nothing

We replace the PubMedBERT text encoder with random Gaussian vectors of matched dimension, removing all domain knowledge and all semantic structure from the conditioning signal. Performance changes by ≈0.000 Dice. We conclude that the query functions as an opaque class identifier that happens to be spelled in English, and that improvements to the text encoder cannot address this failure.

4. Interventions

Three size-aware interventions were evaluated and each reversed after its control: size-conditioned prompting improved the small tercile at the cost of the large; multi-scale ensembling gained on tumour core and lost the gain on enhancing tumour; scale-matched retraining overfit the tercile on which it was tuned.

The model traverses each volume in overlapping sliding windows whose predictions are merged by unweighted mean. For a lesion small relative to the window stride, this disperses probability mass across all windows overlapping the lesion. Reweighting each window by a kernel centred on its own midpoint yields +0.145 Dice with no parameter updates, and recovers non-zero Dice on small enhancing tumour in 7 of 21 patients where the baseline recovered none.

5. Conclusion

A shared confound is not a cancelled confound. Three results in this work were reversed after the fact by controls that should have preceded them. Every stage of a pipeline is an instrument until somebody measures it.
