Text-conditioned 3D medical localization

A Dice score of 0.010 means nothing until you know what it is 0.010 of.

These models detect that a tumour is present, then return a region many times too large and centred somewhere else. The smaller the lesion, the wider the miss, and because detection stays correct at every size an unstratified benchmark never shows it.

Detection holds at 100% while localization falls to 7%. A single aggregate score cannot show that, which is how this survived two years of reported benchmarks.

Sorted by lesion volume into terciles, localization declines monotonically in every region and never recovers. For enhancing tumour — the smallest and most clinically specific region — performance falls 14.9× from the largest third to the smallest.

Four explanations. Three were ruled out.

01

The lesions are unfindable

A plain supervised U-Net, given the same lesions with no language anywhere in the pipeline, barely degrades. The data is not the problem.

Ruled out1.2×

02

The text encoder is too weak

Replacing PubMedBERT with random vectors changes almost nothing. The query is an opaque class identifier that happens to be spelled in English.

Ruled outΔ ≈ 0

03

The model must be told the lesion is small

Size-conditioned prompting, multi-scale ensembling and scale-matched retraining each traded one failure for another.

Ruled out3 of 3

04

The sliding windows are merged badly

Flat averaging smears a small lesion across everything near a window edge. Weight each window toward its own centre.

Confirmed+0.145

The repair

The fault was never in the model. It was in the inference plumbing.

Three more elaborate interventions were tried first and each was reversed after a control caught the error. A shared confound is not a cancelled confound: every stage of a pipeline is an instrument until somebody measures it.

+0.145 Dice, no modified weights. Small enhancing tumour recovered in 7 of 21 patients where the model previously found none.
