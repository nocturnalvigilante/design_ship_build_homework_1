It finds the lesion.

Text-conditioned 3D models detect that a tumour is present and then return a region many times too large. The smaller the lesion, the wider the miss.

1

The lesions are not the problem

A plain supervised U-Net was handed the same small lesions with no language anywhere in the pipeline. It degraded by a factor of 1.2 against 14.9 for the text-conditioned model. The material is learnable. The conditioning is what fails.

The language contributes nothing

PubMedBERT was replaced with random vectors carrying no medical knowledge and no semantics. Performance barely moved. The query functions as an opaque class identifier that happens to be spelled in English.

3

No elaborate remedy survived its control

Size-conditioned prompting helped the small tercile and broke the large. Multi-scale ensembling gained on tumour core and surrendered it on enhancing. Scale-matched retraining overfit the tercile it was tuned on. All three reversed.

Struck out

3 of 3

4

The fault was in the machinery

The volume is traversed in overlapping sliding windows merged by flat average, which smears a small lesion across everything near a window edge. Weight each window toward its own centre. No retraining. No modified weights. Small enhancing tumour recovered in 7 of 21 patients.

Conclusion

A shared confound is not a cancelled confound

The fault was not in the model and not in the language. It sat in the inference plumbing, where nobody had thought to look.

Every stage of a pipeline is an instrument until somebody measures it.
