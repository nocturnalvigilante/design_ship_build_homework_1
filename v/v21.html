It finds the lesion.

How the cause was narrowed

Four explanations. Three of them were wrong.

01

Small lesions are simply unfindable.

A plain supervised U-Net, handed the same lesions with no language anywhere in the pipeline, barely degrades. The lesions are learnable.

Ruled out1.2×

02

The medical text encoder is too weak.

Replacing PubMedBERT with random vectors changes almost nothing. The query is an opaque class identifier that happens to be spelled in English.

Ruled outΔ ≈ 0.000

03

The model must be told the lesion is small.

Size-conditioned prompting, multi-scale ensembling and scale-matched retraining each traded one failure for another.

Ruled out3 of 3 reversed

04

The sliding windows are merged badly.

Flat averaging smears a small lesion across everything near a window edge. Weight each window toward its own centre.

Confirmed+0.145

The repair

The cheapest thing on the list beat every elaborate one. No retraining at all.

The failure was not in the model and not in the language. It sat in the inference plumbing, where nobody was looking.

Three more elaborate interventions were tried first and each was reversed after a control caught the error. A shared confound is not a cancelled confound: every stage of a pipeline is an instrument until somebody measures it.
