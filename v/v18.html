Medical imaging  ·  Vision and language

The model that could not point

Ask a text-conditioned 3D model whether there is a tumour and it will tell you, correctly. Ask it where, and once the lesion is small, it hands back a region many times too large and centred somewhere else entirely.

Raj Hansini Akara · MPCS 53113, University of Chicago BraTS2020 · 369 patients · 128³ volumes

The clinically urgent lesions are the small ones. They are the ones a radiologist might miss on a busy afternoon, the ones that argue for another scan in three months rather than six. They are also, it turns out, precisely the ones these models cannot find.

Not detect — find. The distinction matters more than it sounds. Run the model on a held-out patient with a small enhancing tumour and it will confidently report that a lesion is present. It will then outline a region roughly sixteen times the true volume, its centre a couple of centimetres from where the tumour actually sits.

It is not that small lesions are hard

The obvious objection is that a few voxels of low-contrast tissue with ambiguous borders may simply be beyond finding. So the same small lesions were handed to a plain supervised U-Net, with no language involved anywhere in the pipeline.

It degraded by a factor of 1.2. The text-conditioned model degrades by 14.9 on enhancing tumour over the same range. The lesions are learnable. Something about conditioning on language is what breaks.

Nor is it the language

The next suspicion was the text encoder, and the fix would have been a better one. Instead, PubMedBERT was replaced with random vectors — no medical knowledge, no semantics, nothing at all. Performance barely moved.

The query functions as an opaque class identifier that happens to be spelled in English.

The model is not reading the prompt. It is looking up a channel. Which means no amount of better text, better encoder or better prompt engineering was ever going to repair this.

What was left was the plumbing

Three elaborate interventions were tried first. Size-conditioned prompting helped small lesions and broke large ones. Multi-scale ensembling gained on tumour core and gave the gain back on enhancing tumour. Scale-matched retraining overfit the size tercile it was tuned on. Each was reversed after a control caught the error.

What worked was the least interesting idea available. The model processes volumes in overlapping sliding windows and merges them by flat averaging, which smears a small lesion sitting near a window edge across everything around it. Weight each window toward its own centre instead, and Dice rises by 0.145 with no retraining whatsoever.

What to carry away

A shared confound is not a cancelled confound

Three results here were reversed after the fact, each time by a control that should have been run first. The lesson generalises past this dataset: every stage of a pipeline is an instrument until somebody measures it.
