Plate IV

De Lesione Minima

On the failure of a language-conditioned model to locate that which it has already declared to be present.

Fig. 1. — The predicted region (B) exceeds the true lesion (A) by some sixteen times in volume and lies displaced from it by two centimetres. Both are drawn from the same patient, held out of training.

A.The lesion as it is. Small enhancing tumour, 1.9 cm³, marked by hatching.
B.The lesion as the model reports it. 31.4 cm³, marked by a dotted contour.
C.The contralateral hemisphere, correctly excluded, and the only part of the task the model performs well.
D.The remedy, not figured: each sliding window weighted toward its own centre.

Observationes

The instrument declares, correctly, that a tumour is present. Asked where it lies, it returns a region many times too large and centred elsewhere, and does so worse the smaller the lesion becomes. The decline is monotonic in every region and admits no recovery: for enhancing tumour, 14.9× from the largest size tercile to the smallest.

It was supposed that such lesions might simply be beyond finding. They are not. A supervised network, given the same specimens and no language whatever, degrades by a factor of 1.2. The fault lies in the conditioning.

Nor does the language contribute. Substituting random vectors for the medical text encoder alters almost nothing: the query functions as an opaque class identifier that happens to be spelled in English.

Three elaborate remedies were attempted and each reversed when a control exposed the error. The remedy that held was the plainest: the volume is traversed in overlapping windows and merged by flat average, which disperses a small lesion across all that surrounds it. Weighted toward their centres instead, the windows yield +0.145 Dice with no retraining, and small enhancing tumour is recovered in seven of twenty-one patients where formerly none was found.
