It finds the lesion. It cannot place it.

Text-conditioned 3D medical models detect that a tumour is present, then return a region many times too large and centred somewhere else. The smaller the lesion, the wider the miss — and the small ones are the clinically urgent ones.

Diagnosis — four hypotheses tested
× Small lesions are unfindable. A supervised U-Net given the same lesions, no language anywhere, degrades 1.2×. The lesions are learnable.
× The text encoder is too weak. Replacing PubMedBERT with random vectors changes almost nothing. The query is an opaque class identifier that happens to be spelled in English.
× The model must be told the lesion is small. Size-conditioned prompting, multi-scale ensembling and scale-matched retraining each traded one failure for another. All three reversed.
✓ The sliding windows are merged badly. Flat averaging smears a small lesion across everything near a window edge. Weighting each window toward its own centre gives +0.145 Dice with no retraining, recovering small enhancing tumour in 7 of 21 patients.
Note

A shared confound is not a cancelled confound. Every stage of a pipeline is an instrument until somebody measures it.

171 paired Wilcoxon tests, Benjamini‑Hochberg corrected; 9 of 9 comparisons replicated across three independent splits.
