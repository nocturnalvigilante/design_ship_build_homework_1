Diagnosing small-lesion grounding failure in text-conditioned 3D medical localization

Abstract

Text-conditioned 3D medical models detect that a lesion is present and then fail to localize it once it is small. We show the failure is monotonic in lesion size, that it is not a property of the data, that the language contributes nothing measurable, and that the largest available repair costs no retraining at all.

The diagnosis

A plain supervised U-Net given the same small lesions degrades by a factor of 1.2. The lesions are learnable. The conditioning is what fails.

Fig. 1 — Swapping the domain text encoder for random vectors changes almost nothing.

The query functions as an opaque class identifier that happens to be spelled in English. No better prompt was ever going to fix this.

The repair

03The cheapest intervention won

  • Size-conditioned prompting — helped small, broke large
  • Multi-scale ensembling — gained core, lost enhancing
  • Scale-matched retraining — overfit its own tercile
  • Centre-weighted window merging — +0.145 Dice

The volume is traversed in overlapping sliding windows merged by flat average, which smears a small lesion across everything near a window edge. Weighting each window toward its own centre repairs it.

Three of the four interventions above were reversed after a control caught the error.

Conclusions

A shared confound is not a cancelled confound.

Language conditioning contributes nothing measurable to small-lesion localization in this setting, and the dominant failure lives in the inference plumbing rather than in the model or the data.

Every stage of a pipeline is an instrument until somebody measures it.
