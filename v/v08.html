Scroll to watch it fail

The lesion gets smaller. The guess gets bigger.

Text-conditioned 3D medical models detect that a tumour is present and then outline the wrong place. The smaller the lesion, the wider they miss.

Keep scrolling

01

Ask it whether a tumour is present.

It says yes, and it is right. Detection is not the problem, which is exactly what makes the rest of this misleading if you only look at one number.

02

On a large lesion, it does roughly fine.

Whole tumour, largest size tercile. The outline is loose but it is in the right place, and any aggregate metric will call this acceptable.

0.281 Dice · large

03

Halve the lesion and it starts drifting.

Medium tercile. The predicted region has barely shrunk while the target has, so the overlap falls faster than the lesion does.

0.137 Dice · medium

04

On a small lesion it collapses.

Small enhancing tumour. The prediction is sixteen times the true volume and centred two centimetres away. Dice falls 14.9× from the large tercile, and the decline is monotonic in every region.

0.010 Dice · small ET

05

But the lesion is findable.

A plain supervised U-Net, no text involved, given the same small lesions, degrades by a factor of 1.2. The data is not the problem. Conditioning on language is.

1.2× supervised control

06

And the language is doing nothing.

Replace PubMedBERT with random vectors and almost nothing changes. The query functions as an opaque class identifier that happens to be spelled in English.

≈0.000 Δ Dice

07

The fix cost nothing.

The model merges overlapping sliding windows by flat averaging, which smears a small lesion across everything near a window edge. Weight each window toward its own centre instead: +0.145 Dice, no retraining. Small enhancing tumour goes from 0 to 7 of 21 patients.

+0.145 Dice · no weights changed

Where it leaves us

A shared confound is not a cancelled confound.

Three interventions here were reversed after a control caught the error. Every stage of a pipeline is an instrument until somebody measures it.

171 paired Wilcoxon tests · Benjamini‑Hochberg corrected · 9 of 9 comparisons replicated across three independent splits.
