IT FINDS THE LESION—
IT CAN'T POINT AT IT!

Text-conditioned 3D medical models detect that a tumour is present, then hand back a region many times too large and centred somewhere else. The smaller the lesion, the wider the miss.

Panel 1 — the scene of the crime

Sixteen times the volume. Two centimetres off centre. Dice 0.037. And the detection? Dead right.

“There IS a tumour!”

14.9×

WORSE on small lesions than large. Monotonic in every region, and it never recovers.

Suspect 1 — “the lesions are too small!”

ALIBI CHECKS OUT.

We handed the same lesions to a plain supervised U-Net. No language. No prompt. Nothing clever. It degraded 1.2×.

The lesions are findable. Somebody just wasn't finding them.

Suspect 2 — “the text encoder is weak!”

ALSO INNOCENT.

We pulled PubMedBERT and dropped in random vectors. No medical knowledge. No semantics. Noise.

Nothing changed. The query is an opaque class identifier that happens to be spelled in English.

Suspect 3 — “just tell it the lesion is small!”

THREE FALSE LEADS.

Size-conditioned prompting helped small and broke large. Multi-scale ensembling gained the core and lost the enhancing. Scale-matched retraining overfit its own tercile.

All three reversed once the control was run.

The culprit — the plumbing!

+0.145!

The volume is walked in overlapping windows and the windows are averaged flat, which smears a small lesion across everything it passes.

Weight each window toward its own centre. That's the whole fix. Small enhancing tumour goes from 0 to 7 of 21 patients, and not one weight changed.
