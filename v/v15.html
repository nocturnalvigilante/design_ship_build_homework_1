Four explanations. Three of them were wrong.

Text-conditioned 3D medical models detect that a lesion is present and then fail to localize it once it is small. This is how the cause was narrowed down.

The observation

Dice falls by up to 14.9× from large lesions to small ones, in every region, with no recovery.

1

Perhaps small lesions are simply unfindable.

Test — hand the same small lesions to a plain supervised U-Net, with no language involved at all.

Large0.149
Medium0.037
Small0.010

Enhancing tumour, held-out n = 73. Whole tumour falls 4.9× and tumour core 9.3× over the same range.

Ruled out  1.2× vs 14.9×

The control degrades by a factor of 1.2 on the same lesions. They are learnable. Whatever is broken is not the data.

2

Perhaps the medical text encoder is too weak.

Test — replace PubMedBERT with random vectors and measure what is lost.

Ruled out  Δ Dice ≈ 0.000

Almost nothing is lost, because almost nothing was being used. The query functions as an opaque class identifier that happens to be spelled in English. A better encoder was never going to help.

3

Perhaps the model needs to be told the lesion is small.

Test — size-conditioned prompting, multi-scale ensembling, scale-matched retraining, each measured against its own control.

Ruled out  3 of 3 reversed

Each traded one failure for another: helped small and broke large, gained on tumour core and gave it back on enhancing, or overfit the size tercile it was tuned on. All three were reversed after a control caught the error.

4

Perhaps the windows are simply merged badly.

Test — weight each sliding window toward its own centre instead of averaging them flat.

Confirmed  +0.145 Dice

The volume is traversed in overlapping windows and averaged uniformly, which smears a small lesion across everything near a window edge. Fixing the merge recovers small enhancing tumour in 7 of 21 patients where the model previously found none, and changes no weights at all.

A shared confound is not a cancelled confound.

The largest available repair was not in the model and not in the language. It was in the inference plumbing, where nobody was looking, because every stage of a pipeline is an instrument until somebody measures it.
