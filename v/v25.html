It finds the lesion. It cannot place it.

These models detect that a tumour is present, then return a region many times too large and centred somewhere else. The smaller the lesion, the wider the miss — and the small ones are the clinically urgent ones.

The panel beside this is the whole finding. A single Dice score is unreadable on its own, which is how the failure survived two years of reported benchmarks. Put it next to a reference value and the collapse is obvious.

Tested for

01

Small lesions are simply unfindable

A plain supervised U-Net, given the same lesions with no language anywhere in the pipeline, barely degrades. The lesions are learnable.

Not detected1.2×

02

The medical text encoder is too weak

Replacing PubMedBERT with random vectors changes almost nothing. The query functions as an opaque class identifier that happens to be spelled in English.

Not detectedΔ ≈ 0

03

The model must be told the lesion is small

Size-conditioned prompting, multi-scale ensembling and scale-matched retraining each traded one failure for another. All three reversed once their controls ran.

Not detected3 of 3

04

The sliding windows are merged badly

Flat averaging smears a small lesion across everything near a window edge. Weight each window toward its own centre. No retraining, no modified weights, and small enhancing tumour recovered in 7 of 21 patients.

A shared confound is not a cancelled confound

The fault was not in the model and not in the language. It sat in the inference plumbing, where nobody was looking.
