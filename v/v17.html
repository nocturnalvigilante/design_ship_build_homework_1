Abstract

Text-conditioned 3D medical localization models report strong aggregate performance while failing at the task they are deployed for. We show that localization degrades monotonically with lesion size, by up to 14.9× in Dice from the largest size tercile to the smallest, while detection remains unaffected.

Three controls isolate the cause. A supervised U-Net given the same small lesions degrades only 1.2×, establishing that the lesions are learnable. Replacing the domain text encoder with random vectors changes performance by ≈0.000 Dice: the query functions as an opaque class identifier that happens to be spelled in English. Three size-aware interventions each traded one failure mode for another and were reversed under control.

The dominant failure is instead located in inference-time window merging. Reweighting each sliding window toward its centre yields +0.145 Dice with no retraining, recovering small enhancing tumour in 7 of 21 patients where the model previously found none.

R1

Addressed. A supervised U-Net on the same lesions, with no language conditioning at any stage, degrades 1.2× over the same range.

Excluded1.2×

Addressed. Replacing PubMedBERT with random Gaussian vectors of matched dimension changes performance by ≈0.000 Dice.

Have size-aware training interventions been tried?

Yes, and reported as negative. Size-conditioned prompting, multi-scale ensembling and scale-matched retraining each traded one failure mode for another. All three were reversed under control.
